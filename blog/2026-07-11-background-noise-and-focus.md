**Background noise does not just interrupt you, it quietly taxes your attention even when you think you have tuned it out.** Understanding the cost makes the fixes worth it.

{/* truncate */}

## Noise competes for your attention

Your brain processes sound whether you want it to or not. A conversation nearby, a fan, or traffic all pull a slice of your attention, which is why open offices and noisy homes make deep work harder. You can adapt to steady noise, but sudden or speech-like sounds are especially disruptive because your brain tries to make sense of them.

## On calls, the cost is doubled

In a meeting, noise hits twice. It distracts you, and it distracts everyone listening to you. They spend effort separating your voice from your background, which is the listening fatigue that makes back-to-back calls so draining. See our [statistics on meeting fatigue](/blog/remote-work-meeting-statistics-2026).

## Simple ways to cut the cost

- **For focus:** noise-cancelling headphones reduce the steady noise you hear, making it easier to concentrate.
- **For calls:** noise-cancellation software removes the noise you send, so you are not adding to everyone else's fatigue. Our [guide to removing background noise](/docs/guides/how-to-remove-background-noise) walks through it.
- **For the room:** soft surfaces absorb sound, so a rug and curtains do real work.

## The short version

Noise is a hidden tax on both focus and calls. Headphones help what you hear, noise-cancellation software helps what you send, and a softer room helps both. Small fixes, real difference in how much attention you keep through a day.
