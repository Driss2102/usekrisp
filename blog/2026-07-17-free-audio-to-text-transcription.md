**There are more free transcription options than most people realize, but they trade accuracy and convenience for the zero price tag.** Here are the real free routes and where each one breaks down.

{/* truncate */}

## The free options

- **Built-in dictation.** Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android all have voice typing. Great for short notes, awkward for long recordings.
- **Google Docs Voice Typing.** Free and decent for live speech into a document, but it does not handle an existing audio file well.
- **App captions.** Zoom, Teams, and Meet generate live captions for free, and some plans save the transcript.
- **Free tiers of transcription tools.** Many paid tools include a limited free tier, usually capped by minutes per month.

## Where free stops being enough

Free options tend to struggle with three things:

- **Noisy audio**, which wrecks accuracy across all of them.
- **Speaker labels**, which most free tools do not do well.
- **Volume**, since free tiers cap your minutes.

If your audio is clean, free tools get you surprisingly far. If it is noisy, accuracy falls apart, and no amount of free software fixes a bad recording after the fact. Cleaning the audio during the call is what protects the transcript. See our [guide to transcribing a meeting](/docs/guides/how-to-transcribe-meetings).

## The short version

For clean, short audio, the free built-in tools are enough. For noisy, long, or multi-speaker recordings, the free options show their limits fast, and clean input matters more than which transcriber you pick.
