**The best meeting notes are not a transcript, they are a filter: decisions, actions, and the few things worth remembering.** Here is how to take notes that help future you.

{/* truncate */}

## Capture three things, not everything

You do not need every word. You need:

- **Decisions:** what was agreed.
- **Action items:** who does what by when.
- **Open questions:** what is still unresolved.

If a sentence does not fit one of those three, it usually does not belong in the notes.

## Do not transcribe in your head

The most common note-taking mistake is trying to write down everything as it is said. You end up half-listening and with messy notes. Either capture keywords only and flesh them out right after, or let a tool record and transcribe so you can focus on the discussion. AI note-takers are genuinely useful here, and we cover them in our [AI meeting notes](/docs/guides/ai-meeting-notes) guide.

## Format for the next reader

- Put action items at the top or clearly marked, since that is what people look for.
- Use names next to owners so nobody has to guess.
- Keep it skimmable. Bullets beat paragraphs.

## Review and share

Notes nobody sees are wasted. Share them the same day, and glance at last time's notes before the next meeting so the loop actually closes.

## The short version

Filter for decisions, actions, and open questions. Do not transcribe in your head. Format for the person who reads it later, then share it fast.
