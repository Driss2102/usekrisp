# Hardware vs software noise cancelling

<DisclosureNotice />

**For most people on calls, software noise cancellation is the more effective choice, because it works with any microphone, cleans both your outgoing and incoming audio, and removes other people's background noise too, none of which a hardware mic can do.** Hardware still matters, but it fixes only your own side, at your own end, and it costs more. Here is the honest split.

## What each one actually does

- **Hardware noise cancelling** means the physical setup: a noise-cancelling or cardioid microphone, a headset, and a quieter, treated room. It reduces how much noise reaches your mic in the first place.
- **Software noise cancelling** means a tool like Krisp or NVIDIA Broadcast that processes the audio and strips noise after the mic picks it up, in real time, on any app.

## Where hardware wins

- **It stops noise at the source.** A good mic close to your mouth simply hears less of the room.
- **No processing artifacts.** Nothing is being cleaned up, so nothing can sound over-processed.
- **It helps every tool downstream**, since the input is already cleaner.

The catch: hardware only cleans **your** side, it does nothing about a noisy person on the other end, and a good mic plus a treated room is a real cost.

## Where software wins

- **It cleans both directions.** Krisp removes noise from your mic and from the incoming audio, so a noisy participant stops derailing the call.
- **It works with the gear you already have**, on any laptop, in any calling app.
- **It is cheaper and instant.** No room treatment, no new hardware.

The catch: heavy processing can occasionally sound slightly synthetic on the most extreme noise.

## The practical answer

For a studio recording a single voice, hardware in a treated room is hard to beat. For everyday calls, where the noise is unpredictable and often on the other person's end, **software is more effective in practice**, because it handles both sides on whatever setup you already own. Many people do both: a decent mic, plus software to clean what the mic still catches.
