**Back-to-back meetings are draining because your brain never gets to reset, and you spend each call half-remembering the last one.** A few habits make the day survivable.

{/* truncate */}

## Protect the gaps

- **Book meetings for 25 or 50 minutes**, not 30 or 60. The few minutes between are for water, notes, and a breath.
- **Decline what you can.** If you are not needed to decide, ask for the notes instead.

## Reduce the mental load per call

- **Let something else take notes.** If you are transcribing in your head, you are not listening. Automatic notes free you to pay attention and still walk away with a record.
- **Clean up your audio once.** Fumbling with mic settings at the top of every call wastes the gap you just protected. Set noise cancellation once and forget it.

## Stay present

- **Close the other tabs.** Nothing signals "I am not listening" like obvious multitasking, and it also means you miss things and have to circle back.
- **Write the one decision** from each call before the next starts. It takes ten seconds and saves you re-attending the meeting in your head later.

## The short version

Shorten meetings to create gaps, offload note-taking so you can actually listen, and set your audio once so every call just works. The goal is to end the day with clear notes and some energy left, instead of a blur of calls you half remember.
