**Most action items fail for one reason: nobody can tell exactly who is doing what by when.** Fix that and follow-through jumps.

{/* truncate */}

## What a good action item looks like

Three parts, every time:

1. **A verb and a clear outcome.** "Send the draft," not "the draft."
2. **One named owner.** Not a team, a person. Shared ownership is no ownership.
3. **A due date.** "By Friday," not "soon."

Example: "Priya sends the revised proposal to the client by Thursday." You can tell instantly whether it happened.

## Capture them in the moment

Action items written from memory after the meeting are always weaker than ones captured as they are said. If you rely on recall, you lose the exact wording and the owner. Tools that detect and record action items during the call solve this, and it is one of the things AI note-takers are genuinely good at. See our guide on [AI meeting minutes](/docs/guides/ai-meeting-minutes).

## Review them before the next meeting

An action item list that is never revisited trains everyone to ignore it. Open last meeting's list at the start of the next one. Two minutes of accountability keeps the list from becoming decoration.

## The short version

Verb, owner, date. Capture them live so nothing is lost, and review them next time so they mean something. That is the whole system, and it works.
