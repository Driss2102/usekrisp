**Not everyone has a quiet home office. Most people have kids, pets, housemates, or a street outside the window.** Here are seven fixes that work in real homes, ordered from free to most effective.

{/* truncate */}

## 1. Pick your room by its softness, not its size

A small room with a bed, a rug, and curtains sounds better than a big empty one. Soft things absorb sound, hard things bounce it.

## 2. Face away from the noise source

Point your mic away from the window, the door, or the hallway. Directional mics pick up what is in front of them, so turn the noise behind you.

## 3. Wear headphones

This removes echo and stops your speakers from feeding back into the call. It is the cheapest upgrade there is.

## 4. Use a close mic

A headset or a mic near your mouth hears you loudly and the room quietly. Distance is the enemy.

## 5. Schedule around the chaos

If the household is loudest at certain times, book your important calls around them where you can. Not always possible, but it helps.

## 6. Turn on built-in noise suppression

Zoom, Teams, and Meet all have it. Switch it on. It handles light, steady noise reasonably well.

## 7. Add real-time noise cancellation for the hard cases

When the noise is loud, sudden, or constant, built-in tools struggle. Dedicated software cleans your audio in real time, on any app, and even cleans a noisy person on the other end. This is the fix that lets you take a call during nap time or with traffic outside. See [Krisp for remote work](/docs/for/remote-work) for how that fits a home setup.

## The bottom line

You cannot always control your home, but you can control what reaches the call. Soft room, close mic, headphones, and real-time noise cancellation together let you sound like you are in a quiet office, even when you are not.
