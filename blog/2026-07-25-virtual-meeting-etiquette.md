**Most virtual meeting etiquette advice is either obvious or outdated.** Here are the rules that genuinely make calls better for everyone.

{/* truncate */}

## Rules that matter

- **Mute when you are not talking**, or use noise cancellation so your background does not intrude. Nobody needs to hear your dog, your street, or your keyboard.
- **Show up on time and end on time.** Running over is the most common way meetings steal people's day.
- **Look at the camera for key moments.** You do not have to stare, but eye contact when you make a point lands better.
- **Do not multitask visibly.** If your eyes are clearly elsewhere, people notice, and you miss things.
- **Let people finish.** Remote audio has a slight delay, so talking over each other is worse on video than in person. Leave a beat.

## Rules that are overrated

- **Camera always on.** Sometimes audio-only is the considerate choice, especially on a bad connection or a long call.
- **Rigid hand-raising for everything.** Useful in big meetings, unnecessary in a three-person call.

## The audio rule people forget

Etiquette is not just behavior, it is being audible. Showing up with clean, clear audio is a courtesy: it means people are not straining to understand you or asking you to repeat yourself. If your space is noisy, noise cancellation is the polite fix. See our [guide to removing background noise](/docs/guides/how-to-remove-background-noise).

## The short version

Be audible, be on time, be present, and let people finish. The rest is preference. Good etiquette is mostly respect for other people's attention and ears.
