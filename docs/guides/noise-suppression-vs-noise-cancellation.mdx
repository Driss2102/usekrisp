# Noise suppression vs noise cancellation

<DisclosureNotice />

**People use "noise suppression" and "noise cancellation" to mean almost the same thing, but there is a useful distinction.** Getting it right helps you pick the correct fix for noisy calls.

## The quick definitions

- **Noise cancellation** classically refers to hardware that cancels sound using opposing sound waves, like the active noise cancellation in headphones. It reduces the noise **you hear**.
- **Noise suppression** usually refers to software that removes unwanted sound from a microphone signal. It reduces the noise **you send** on a call.

In everyday use the terms overlap, and software tools often call their microphone cleaning "noise cancellation" too. What matters is which direction is being cleaned.

## Which one do you need for calls?

For meetings, the problem is almost always what your microphone sends, so you want **software noise suppression** on your mic. If you also want quieter listening, add **noise-cancelling headphones**. The two are complementary.

The strongest tools do both directions: they suppress the noise from your microphone and clean the incoming audio, so a noisy person on the other end stops derailing the call.
